- **Results of those tests:** `notes/MRDT_secondary_results.md`, Figs 5–6.
  - **H1, proximal Chr 1:** the haplotype changes the level of mortality, not MRDT. On high fat, B strains die at about twice the rate of D strains at 600 d.
  - **H2, F1 hybrids:** F1 MRDT is shorter, not longer. The effect sits in the short-by-short crosses (98 d against 156 d in their parents). The F1s are buffered against mortality at every age until about 1,000 d, but the advantage narrows with age.
- **Wright's effective-factors question:** `Models/effective_factors.R` and `Models/fig_effective_factors.R`; outputs `output/Model_effective_factors.*`.
  - A simulation with 10 or 200 recessive loci, where load either multiplies the aging hazard or adds its own deaths.
  - **Which F1 type gains more** is set by how load acts on mortality, not by the number of loci.
  - **How large and how uniform the gains are** is set by the number of loci.
  - **The data fit a few effective factors, with load adding its own deaths.** In this model, the long MRDT of short-lived inbreds is a sign of load, not of slower aging.
- **UM-HET3 Vita1a, level against slope:** `UMHET3/extract_vita1a_deaths.py` and `UMHET3/vita1a_gompertz.R`.
  - They read Rob's unpublished expanded Table 1 (`00_Table_1_Vita_Loci_Expanded_Version_21Feb2026.xlsx`), which is not stored here. Run them locally for the numbers.
  - **Result:** the paternal D haplotype has the shorter MRDT, and its disadvantage relative to H grows with age to about 1,100 d. Maternal C against B makes little difference.

## Next analyses

1. **Gompertz–Makeham, or a two-part fit, on the short-lived parents against the short-by-short F1s.** Do the parents carry an extra component that rises little with age, and do the slopes converge once it is modeled?
2. **Lifespan against MRDT across all BXD strains.** Take each from separate halves of each strain's mice, so the level–slope coupling in the fit does not create a correlation. If load adds deaths, short-lived strains should have longer MRDTs.
3. **Arends Supplementary Data Table 2** (in `Arends_Supplementary_Tables_10Apr2026.zip`). It gives deaths per 15 days for all 29 Vita loci by sex and genotype (CH, CD, BH, BD), with numbers alive and genotyped.
   - Fit level and MRDT for every locus by sex. This is a principled redo of the "RAM loci effects" sheet, which is marked "REDO COMPLETELY": a rate-of-aging modulator is a locus that changes MRDT.
   - Also check the Vita1a puzzles in Table 1f. The columns are labeled CH, BH, BD, CD, but their hazards match Table 1b only in the order CH, CD, BH, BD. The counts are 2:1, paternal H to paternal D. It is unclear which 3,133 mice, and which sex, the table covers.
4. **Founder variants** (Supplementary Table 17, 629 MB): collapse the four haplotypes into B-like and D-like at sites where B6 and D2 differ.
   - Only sites where BALB/c carries the D allele and C3H the B allele give all three genotypes in UM-HET3, so only there can additive effects and dominance be estimated.
   - **Query for Vita1a (3–8 Mb):** sites where C3H differs from B6 = BALB/c = D2, and sites where D2 differs from the other three. The BXDs favor a protective C3H allele over a harmful D2 allele.
   - **Query for Vita1b (16–31 Mb, peak 24.0 Mb):** sites where B6 = D2 differs from BALB/c = C3H. BD is the only bad genotype, which points to a recessive allele shared by B6 and D2. Such an allele cannot segregate in the BXDs.
