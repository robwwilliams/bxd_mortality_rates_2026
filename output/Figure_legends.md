# Figure legends: initial Gompertz analysis of MRDT by diet

Figures are in this folder as SVG (editable text, Arial) and PNG previews.

**Figure 1. Mortality rises in parallel on chow and high-fat diets.** Mortality rate (deaths per 1,000 mouse-days, log scale) against age for chow-fed (CD, blue, n = 1,044) and high-fat-fed (HF, orange, n = 890) BXD family females. Points are occurrence/exposure rates in 30-day age intervals: deaths in the interval divided by mouse-days at risk, each mouse counted from its age at set up. Intervals are shown where at least 20 mice were at risk; point diameter grows with the square root of the number of deaths. Lines are Gompertz fits by maximum likelihood, left-truncated at set-up age, drawn from 100 to 1,250 d. The key gives each diet's MRDT with its 95% Wald interval. The slopes do not differ (likelihood-ratio test, p = 0.55). With a shared slope (MRDT 151 d), HF mortality is 1.42 times CD mortality at every age (95% CI 1.29–1.55).

**Figure 2. Survival from 200 days.** Kaplan–Meier survival (solid lines) of mice alive at 200 d (CD n = 1,018, HF n = 865), each at risk from the later of its set-up age and 200 d. Dashed lines show survival predicted by the primary Gompertz fits (Figure 1), conditional on survival to 200 d. Labels give median age at death among these mice. The Gompertz curve fits CD closely; for HF, observed mortality is higher than predicted from about 600 to 850 d and lower after that.

**Figure 3. MRDT is robust to the choice of analysis.** Mortality rate doubling time (days) for CD (blue) and HF (orange) under the primary analysis and five alternatives (rows; definitions in section 5). Points are estimates and bars are 95% intervals. The intervals are Wald intervals on the ln b scale, except in two rows: the bootstrap row uses the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of 2,000 resamples, and the binned-regression row uses the regression standard error of the slope. At right is the likelihood-ratio p value for equal slopes in the four analyses where it was computed. Only the analysis restricted to mice surviving to 365 d gives a nominally different slope (p = 0.035).

**Figure 4. Intercept and slope estimates trade off along a ridge.** Each small point is one of 2,000 bootstrap fits per diet (mice resampled with replacement within diet), plotting ln(R0) = ln(a) against MRDT. Large points are the estimates from the full data. Within each diet the estimates lie on a narrow diagonal ridge: ln(a) and ln(b) correlate at −0.95 (CD) and −0.97 (HF), the Strehler–Mildvan degeneracy of the Gompertz fit. The two ridges overlap in MRDT but are separated along ln(R0), showing that the diets differ in age-independent mortality, not in the rate of aging. Because of the ridge, slopes should be compared with the joint likelihood-ratio test rather than by comparing separate intervals.
