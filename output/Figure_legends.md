# Figure legends: Gompertz analyses of MRDT by diet (Figs 1–4) and the pre-registered secondary tests (Figs 5–6)

Figures are in this folder as SVG (editable text, Arial) and PNG previews. Colors: chow (CD) brown; high fat (HF, the "blue diet") blue. In Fig. 6, haplotype colors follow Arends et al. 2026: B strains dark purple, D strains orange. Inbred parents are pink and F1 hybrids green. Legend style follows Nature (see notes/figure_style.md).

**Fig. 1 | A high-fat diet raises mortality at every age without changing the mortality rate doubling time.** Mortality rates of BXD family females fed chow (CD, brown; n = 1,044) or a high-fat diet (HF, blue; n = 890) as a function of age. Points show occurrence/exposure rates in 30-day intervals: deaths per 1,000 mouse-days at risk, with each mouse at risk from its age at set-up. Intervals are shown where at least 20 mice were at risk; point diameter scales with the square root of the number of deaths. Lines show maximum-likelihood Gompertz fits, left-truncated at set-up age and drawn over the ages covered by the data. The y axis is log2-scaled, so each gridline marks one doubling of mortality. The key gives the mortality rate doubling time (MRDT) with 95% Wald confidence intervals (CI). Slopes do not differ between diets (likelihood-ratio test, P = 0.55). With a shared slope (MRDT 151 days), HF mortality is 1.42-fold that of CD (95% CI 1.29–1.55), or 0.50 doublings higher, at every age.

**Fig. 2 | Survival and actuarial survivorships by diet.** a, Kaplan–Meier survival of mice alive at 200 days (CD n = 1,018; HF n = 865), each at risk from the later of its set-up age and 200 days. Dashed lines show survival predicted by the Gompertz fits in Fig. 1, conditional on survival to 200 days; median ages at death are given for each diet. b, Mean age at death of each diet's survivorship relative to both diets combined, as a function of the minimum inclusion age (T-age), in 15-day steps (Arends et al. 2026, Fig. 1b). Each survivorship includes the mice alive and under observation at that T-age. Lines indicate mean and shaded bands represent s.e.m. Lines end where either diet falls below 20 mice (T935); numbers at top give mice alive (CD / HF). HF survivors die 65–69 days earlier than CD survivors up to T400. The difference narrows between T650 and T850, faster than the Gompertz fits predict, and reverses after T860, when few HF mice remain.

**Fig. 3 | Estimates of the mortality rate doubling time are robust to analytical choices.** MRDT (days) for CD (brown) and HF (blue) in the primary analysis and five alternatives (Methods). Points indicate estimates and bars indicate 95% CI: Wald intervals on the log scale of the slope, except in the bootstrap row (2.5th to 97.5th percentiles of 2,000 resamples) and the binned-regression row (regression standard error). P values at right are from likelihood-ratio tests of equal slopes. Only the subset of mice surviving to 365 days gives a nominally different slope (P = 0.035, uncorrected for the four tests).

**Fig. 4 | Estimates of R0 and the mortality rate doubling time are tightly coupled.** Each small point is one of 2,000 bootstrap fits per diet, with mice resampled with replacement within diet; large points are full-data estimates. R0 is the age-independent mortality, in deaths per 1,000 mouse-days at age 0 (log2 scale). Estimates of R0 and MRDT correlate at r = 0.95 (CD) and r = 0.97 (HF), the Strehler–Mildvan degeneracy of the Gompertz fit. The two diets overlap in MRDT and separate along R0. Slopes should therefore be compared with joint likelihood-ratio tests rather than by comparing separate intervals.

**Fig. 5 | The F1 hybrids of short-lived BXD strains have a shorter mortality rate doubling time than their parents; the proximal Chr 1 haplotype does not change it.** Ratio of the mortality rate doubling time (MRDT) of the second group to that of the first (log2 scale) for each comparison. Values left of 1 mean a steeper rise of mortality with age in the second group. The MRDTs (second / first, days) are at right. Females surviving to 200 days, each at risk from the later of its set-up age and 200 days. Each group has one MRDT, shared by its diets; each group × diet cell has its own mortality level. Points indicate estimates. Bars indicate 95% CI from a delete-one-strain jackknife (delete one genotype for the F1 comparisons), except for S2, which has four genotypes and uses a Wald interval. The two registered tests (H1, B strains n = 483 from 33 strains, D strains n = 459 from 34; H2, parents n = 218 from 8 strains, F1 n = 320 from 13 F1 types) give P from t = log2 ratio / jackknife s.e., two-tailed for H1 and one-tailed (F1 MRDT longer) for H2. Each was tested at α = 0.025. Secondary analyses (S1–S6) were specified in advance and are shown as estimates only. Open points are exploratory refits, made after the registered results were seen, that give each genotype × diet cell its own level.

**Fig. 6 | F1 hybrids start with lower mortality and lose their advantage with age; D and B strains differ in level, not slope.** Mortality rates as a function of age for D and B strains on chow (a) and high fat (b), and for F1 hybrids and their inbred parents on chow (c) and high fat (d). Points show occurrence/exposure rates in 60-day intervals, in deaths per 1,000 mouse-days at risk, where at least 10 mice were at risk. Point diameter scales with the square root of the number of deaths. Lines show the registered model: one MRDT per group, shared by both diets, and a level per group and diet, drawn over the ages covered by the data. The y axis is log2-scaled, so each gridline marks one doubling of mortality. n in the keys is the number of females alive at 200 days.
